# About Don & Doff Co.

## The name

Somewhere in NASA's human spaceflight standards there is a requirement numbered V2 11001. Its title is "Suited Donning and Doffing."

It says that spacesuits must allow for "efficient and effective donning and doffing," in normal operations and in emergencies. The wording is plain: suits need to be designed so they can be put on and taken off well.

Before a launch or a walk on another world, someone has to get dressed. Afterward, someone has to come home and take it all off. NASA's own description of EVA preparation runs from unstowing the suit through checkout, donning, doffing, and stowage. The suit is the first thing a crew puts on and the last thing it takes off.

Don is the leaving. Doff is the coming back. We named a clothing brand after the whole cycle.

## Where it started: Gemini

The idea is older than the standard. A 1966 NASA Technical Note (TN D-3291) describes the suit built for the Gemini program, and it reads like a problem about clothing.

The Gemini capsule was too small for a crew member to put on or take off the conventional suit fully in flight. So the design priority became long-term comfort. Engineers built a soft suit that served mostly as a flight suit, and its torso garment was worn throughout the mission. You stepped into it through a pressure-sealing zipper. The helmet had a quick doff-and-don capability, and the document describes an approach followed to simplify donning and doffing and to improve the suit's operational use.

We like that. At its core, one of the real engineering problems in spaceflight was how a person gets in and out of the thing that protects them.

## The same problem, decades later

It never went away. A 2017 NASA Office of Inspector General report on spacesuit development describes a next-generation life support system sized for 100 EVAs, with 8 hours per EVA plus 2 hours of suit donning and doffing built into the budget. Even the life support hardware has to account for the time spent getting in and out.

## What we make

We make clothes for people who understand that how you suit up matters, and that every departure assumes a return. Our designs borrow the language of the suit: the seams, the layers, the ritual of putting something on with intent. We borrow the idea, not the engineering. These are garments, not flight hardware.

*Suit up. Reach orbit. Repeat.*
